# SUPER SAGE OPERATOR — DECISION DEMAND CORPUS V1

Source: real fantasy-football live-chat questions supplied by product owner, 2026-10-05.
Purpose: identify WHY humans seek decision help. These are demand archetypes, not truth labels and not production rules.

## Core observation
Users often possess relevant facts but cannot reconcile competing evidence into an action. The Operator's job is to recognize the conflict, reason across it consistently, make the call, explain why the losing case did not win, and state what would reopen the decision.

## Archetypes

1. ESTABLISHED BASELINE VS CHALLENGER
Human signal: "Do we give Monty one more chance over B Allen or Tuten?"
Conflict: proven/established prior vs recent performance/emerging alternative.
Operator lesson: poor recent fantasy results challenge confidence before they invalidate an established baseline; structural evidence can accelerate reassessment.

2. MATCHUP VS PLAYER QUALITY
Human signal: "not sure who to put for flex based off of mostly matchup"
Conflict: stronger player prior vs apparently easier opponent.
Operator lesson: matchup can narrow a gap; it should not automatically erase a valid baseline.

3. TEAMMATE STATE CHANGE / ROLE REDISTRIBUTION
Human signal: "Monangai if Swift is ruled out..."
Conflict: old workload evidence may no longer describe the player's expected role.
Operator lesson: verified teammate absence is a structural event. Re-open the decision and establish redistribution rather than extrapolating the old role.

4. OPPONENT DEFENSIVE STATE CHANGE
Human signal: "Should I play Coker, Nabers, Adams or Moore with Christian Gonzalez out?"
Conflict: historical/current defense baseline vs changed defensive personnel.
Operator lesson: opponent injuries/returns/trades/scheme changes can weaken or invalidate historical matchup assumptions.

5. CONDITIONAL AVAILABILITY
Human signal: "if Jetta plays..."
Conflict: recommendation depends on a future status branch.
Operator lesson: produce explicit IF/THEN decision branches rather than false static certainty.

6. KICKOFF OPTIONALITY
Human signal: questionable player with alternatives at 9:30, 4:05 and 4:25.
Conflict: player value vs value of preserving a later substitution.
Operator lesson: unresolved availability plus kickoff sequence creates option value; waiting can itself have expected decision value.

7. CURRENT STATE VS HISTORICAL REPUTATION
Human signal: current defense/player behavior conflicts with reputation.
Conflict: long-run prior vs fresh season evidence.
Operator lesson: history establishes the prior; verified structural/current evidence determines whether it still governs.

8. SMALL SAMPLE / REPRESENTATIVENESS
Human signal: "That Falcons RB against stat is fake, they played GB."
Conflict: observed statistic vs whether its sample represents defensive ability.
Operator lesson: inspect sample/opponent quality and causal relevance before allowing a statistic to move belief.

9. OPPORTUNITY VS REALIZED PRODUCTION
Human signal: Mark Andrews has target/YPRR evidence but repeatedly fails to produce.
Conflict: healthy underlying opportunity vs persistent disappointing outcomes.
Operator lesson: one miss is variance; repeated misses plus supporting role/skill evidence can become baseline-revision evidence.

10. FLOOR VS CEILING / UPSIDE
Human signal: volatile breakout candidate vs stable veteran.
Conflict: probability of usable outcome vs magnitude of upside.
Operator lesson: describe the distributional tradeoff; do not equate ceiling with expected value.

11. SCORING / CROSS-POSITION FLEX
Human signal: RB vs WR/TE in half-PPR/full-PPR FLEX.
Conflict: unlike roles and positions must be reconciled under league scoring.
Operator lesson: scoring and lineup legality are decision context, not afterthoughts.

12. MULTI-PLAYER CONSTRAINT
Human signal: "pick 2", "sit one", "need two flexes".
Conflict: decision is portfolio/lineup optimization rather than independent pairwise ranking.
Operator lesson: solve the actual roster constraint; don't answer each player in isolation.

13. COMPETITIVE STATE / COACHING INCENTIVE
Human signal: "if this is a must win for the Pats should I go Maye?"
Conflict: narrative urgency may or may not alter deployment.
Operator lesson: competitive state is CONTEXT_ONLY until a defensible path exists from team incentive -> coaching behavior -> player opportunity.

14. RUSHING / MULTIPLE PRODUCTION PATHS
Human signal: mobile QB or dual-role RB has more than one way to score.
Conflict: passing/rushing or rushing/receiving paths change floor/ceiling.
Operator lesson: independent production paths are legitimate evidence; avoid double-counting correlated manifestations of the same role.

15. TRUST EROSION / RECENCY EMOTION
Human signal: "I can't trust Coker anymore"; "I know whoever I pick it's gonna be wrong."
Conflict: emotional response to recent outcomes vs process evidence.
Operator lesson: acknowledge the reason for hesitation; distinguish rational baseline revision from outcome-driven recency bias.

16. SYSTEM / ANALYST CONTRADICTION
Human signal: one assistant says Downs over Tuten while Auto-Pilot reverses it and another surface says Tuten 93%.
Conflict: same ecosystem appears to give incompatible answers.
Operator lesson: one shared decision authority and evidence state should produce a consistent call; contradictions require explanation, not another percentage.

17. EVIDENCE / EXPLANATION CONSISTENCY
Human signal: "how do you say start Bears DEF then say both offenses will score at a high clip?"
Conflict: recommendation appears inconsistent with stated game thesis.
Operator lesson: explanation must be generated from the evidence that actually decided the call; surface contradictions explicitly.

18. ROSTER / SEASON OBJECTIVE
Human signal: "I'm 0-3"; "other owner needs a win and I'm 3-0."
Conflict: player truth is unchanged, but utility/risk tolerance may differ.
Operator lesson: team state must never alter factual player evaluation; it may affect which outcome distribution best serves the manager's stated objective.

## Explanation contract
For a difficult decision, customer-facing reasoning should answer:
1. THE CALL — who starts.
2. WHY — strongest decision-active evidence.
3. WHY NOT THE OTHER PLAYER — strongest losing case and why it did not cross the threshold.
4. CONFIDENCE — calibrated uncertainty, never manufactured certainty.
5. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE CALL — explicit reopening conditions.

## Training rule
These archetypes teach the Operator what conflict to recognize. They do NOT encode the supplied human questions as correct answers. Expert Decision Labs formulate candidate reasoning grammar; historical validation and Turbine #6 determine whether a rule deserves promotion.
