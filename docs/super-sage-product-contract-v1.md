# Super SAGE Product Contract V1

## Customer promise

Super SAGE behaves like a fantasy-football player with 100 years of experience and an advanced data-scientist mindset. It consumes the complicated evidence for the customer and compresses it into clear, concise, consistent 1/3/10 decision intelligence. Deeper analytical evidence remains available on demand.

## Release requirements

1. **Decide first.** Lead with the recommended lineup.
2. **Explain non-obvious calls.** Name the starter and the strongest real alternative and explain why the decision was made.
3. **Quantify when evidence exists.** For a non-obvious comparison, surface the relevant Weekly SAGE standing/tier, sourced projection, observed recent opportunity, established-role description, and matchup when those fields are present in the frozen decision record.
4. **Separate observation from forecast.** Observed workload describes an established role. It is never presented as expected future workload unless validated forward evidence supports that inference.
5. **State uncertainty.** A provisional/unresolved decision must explicitly say there is no validated edge and why the evidence cannot yet resolve the comparison.
6. **Surface material state changes.** Injury/availability, quarterback environment, verified role change, IR/unavailability, and source conflicts that materially affect the decision belong in the initial 1/3/10 answer.
7. **1/3/10.** One second: lineup and decision state. Three seconds: the few reasons that actually drove the important calls. Ten seconds: enough evidence to understand the close/non-obvious decisions and what would change them.
8. **Deeper on demand.** The initial answer is a synthesis, not an evidence dump. The full record remains available for analytical follow-up.
9. **Bench is not drop.** Weekly start/sit value and roster value remain separate.
10. **Conservative truth.** Missing evidence is identified as missing. The presenter never invents an advanced metric, projection, role, or causal claim.

## Release gate

A release does not pass merely because the decision engine is deterministic or MCP/website parity is green. The literal customer-facing answer generated from the canonical fixture must satisfy this contract.

For a controversial FLEX decision, the acceptance test must prove that, when those fields exist in the decision record, the answer exposes:
- starter and strongest alternative;
- standing/tier;
- projection;
- observed recent workload;
- established role;
- matchup;
- validated/provisional state;
- why the call remains unresolved or decided;
- what could change it.

The presenter may select and compress evidence. It may not re-rank, re-score, or re-decide.
