# Pat vs. Grasshopper — The SAGE Challenge

## The Memorial Kickoff Season, Year 1

**Two competitors. Fifteen weeks. One shared player pool.**

The objective is to win.

The purpose is to learn.

There are many ways to win fantasy football. This experiment is not designed to prove that one philosophy is universally correct. It is designed to discover which decision patterns most consistently increase the probability of winning, under what conditions, and why.

Pat and Grasshopper will disagree. They will make mistakes. They will get lucky. They will get unlucky. None of those things will be hidden by hindsight.

> **Every decision is evidence. Every disagreement is a learning opportunity. The scoreboard determines the winner. The evidence teaches SAGE.**

---

## Governing Principle

> **SAGE never learns that a decision was good simply because it won. Evidence earns the right to change what SAGE believes.**

Winning and good decision-making are related, but they are not identical. A strong decision can lose because of variance. A weak decision can win because of variance. The challenge must preserve that distinction.

The scoreboard determines who won the fantasy competition. The evidence determines what SAGE should learn from it.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Pat vs. Grasshopper SAGE Challenge is a controlled, season-long fantasy-football decision experiment designed to:

1. Test competing fantasy-football decision philosophies in real weekly conditions.
2. Capture the reasoning behind every meaningful decision before outcomes are known.
3. Separate process quality from outcome quality.
4. Identify repeatable decision patterns that improve expected fantasy value and win probability.
5. Surface disagreements, counterintuitive cases, opportunity costs, and failure modes worthy of deeper SAGE research.
6. Create a permanent evidence set that can later be replayed, backtested, simulated, and compared with future SAGE versions.

This is competition with a research purpose. Neither Pat's experience nor Grasshopper's analysis is treated as truth by default. Each decision must earn its standing through evidence.

---

## 2. Competition Format

Year 1 consists of **15 weekly head-to-head challenges** between Pat and Grasshopper.

The inaugural challenge begins with **NFL Week 2 = Challenge Week 1**.

Each week uses one **shared NFL player pool** and a snake-draft structure. The two competitors construct opposing fantasy lineups from that same pool under the same scoring and roster rules. Once a player is drafted by either competitor, that player is unavailable to the other competitor for that week's challenge. **Duplicate players are not permitted.**

### Year 1 Starting Lineup

Each competitor drafts exactly:

- **1 QB**
- **2 RB**
- **2 WR**
- **1 TE**
- **2 FLEX** — each FLEX may be filled by an **RB, WR, or TE**
- **1 PK** (placekicker)
- **1 DEF** (team defense/special teams)

That is **10 starters per competitor and 20 total selections each challenge week**.

The weekly 1.01 alternates between Pat and Grasshopper so neither competitor owns the structural advantage across the full season. **Grasshopper owns the inaugural 1.01 in Challenge Week 1.** Pat owns the 1.01 in Challenge Week 2, and the right continues to alternate through Challenge Week 15.

The **35+ points allowed = -4** DEF tier is also intentional. A severely outscored defense must be capable of materially hurting a lineup, preserving meaningful defensive matchup and selection decisions.

### Year 1 Constitution Freeze

The competition format, lineup configuration, player-pool rules, FLEX eligibility, scoring table, draft-order method, and founding research methodology above are the **frozen Year 1 constitution**.

Once Challenge Week 1 begins, the founding methodology is not casually rewritten. Weekly records, decision evidence, outcomes, learning cases, and scoreboard results are appended as the season progresses.

A correction or amendment may be made only when Pat and Grasshopper explicitly agree that one is required. Every amendment must be documented in this file with:

- the exact rule or methodology changed;
- the effective challenge week;
- the reason for the change; and
- confirmation that historical records remain preserved rather than rewritten.

An amendment must never be applied retroactively merely because later outcomes make a different rule or methodology look preferable.

## 3. Weekly Draft Mechanics

For every week:

- A common eligible NFL player pool is established before drafting begins.
- The competitor holding the 1.01 makes the first selection.
- Selections then proceed in snake order until both 10-player starting lineups are complete.
- A drafted player is removed from the shared pool and cannot be selected by the opponent.
- Grasshopper owns the Challenge Week 1 1.01; the 1.01 alternates every challenge week thereafter.
- Every selection is recorded in sequence.
- Reasoning is captured at decision time, before game outcomes are known.

The purpose of the snake format is not merely competitive balance. Each selection changes the opportunity set available to the opponent, making positional scarcity, replacement value, optionality, roster construction, and opportunity cost observable parts of the experiment.

---

## 4. Decision Record

Every selection should capture enough context to reconstruct the decision later without hindsight.

At minimum, record:

- Week
- Competitor
- Pick number
- Player selected
- NFL team
- Position
- Opponent / matchup context
- Primary decision reason
- Secondary decision reason, when applicable
- Best alternative player considered
- Why the selected player was preferred to the alternative
- Relevant roster-construction context
- Relevant positional-scarcity or opportunity-cost context
- Pregame projection or SAGE evidence available at the time
- Pregame reasoning in plain language
- Final fantasy points
- Weekly result
- Post-outcome decision-quality evaluation
- Whether the case creates a SAGE learning case

The record should preserve what was known **at the time of the decision**. Later information must never be silently inserted into the original reasoning.

## 5. Standard Decision-Reason Vocabulary

Year 1 should use a standardized reason-code vocabulary so patterns can be analyzed across weeks while still preserving free-text reasoning.

Initial reason families include:

- **PROJECTED_VALUE** — strongest expected fantasy output among viable choices.
- **MATCHUP** — opponent or game environment materially improves or reduces expected value.
- **ROLE_VOLUME** — expected snaps, touches, targets, routes, carries, or other opportunity indicators.
- **POSITIONAL_SCARCITY** — value of securing a position before the remaining pool deteriorates.
- **ROSTER_CONSTRUCTION** — selection improves lineup balance or fills a structural need.
- **INCREMENTAL_STARTER_VALUE** — player improves the likely starting lineup more than available alternatives.
- **FLEX_OPTIONALITY** — selection preserves or improves future lineup flexibility.
- **OPPORTUNITY_COST** — passing on the player creates greater expected loss than passing on alternatives.
- **CEILING** — upside distribution is intentionally favored.
- **FLOOR** — stability or downside protection is intentionally favored.
- **CORRELATION** — lineup interaction or game correlation materially affects the choice.
- **INJURY_CONTEXT** — injury status or replacement-role implications materially affect the choice.
- **TREND** — recent role or performance movement is considered meaningful evidence.
- **MARKET_DISAGREEMENT** — decision intentionally differs from consensus, rankings, projection, or opponent philosophy.
- **BLOCK / DENIAL** — selection has strategic value because it removes an especially valuable option from the opponent's future pool.
- **OTHER** — evidence-based rationale not represented above; explanation required.

The vocabulary may evolve when Year 1 exposes useful distinctions, but codes should not be retroactively changed merely to make past decisions look cleaner.

## 6. Alternative Player and Opportunity-Cost Capture

A selection cannot be understood only by evaluating the player who was chosen.

Every meaningful decision should identify the strongest realistic alternative available at that moment. The experiment should ask:

**What did this selection gain, and what did choosing it force the competitor to give up?**

This is central to SAGE. Fantasy decisions are relative decisions made under constrained availability. A player can be excellent in isolation and still be the wrong selection if another available choice creates more incremental lineup value or if equivalent value at the chosen position is likely to remain available later.

---

## 7. Matchup Reasoning

Matchup information is evidence, not an automatic override.

When matchup materially affects a selection, the decision record should identify what specifically matters: defensive strength, coverage tendency, expected game script, pace, scoring environment, offensive-line matchup, injury-driven role changes, weather when relevant, or another defensible mechanism.

Generic statements such as "good matchup" should not be sufficient for a SAGE learning case without supporting context.

---

## 8. Pregame Lock and Hindsight Protection

All selections, alternatives, reason codes, and pregame reasoning are locked before the relevant outcomes occur.

Postgame analysis may challenge the reasoning, but it may not rewrite it.

If new information emerges after a decision but before kickoff — for example, an injury designation, inactive announcement, weather event, or depth-chart change — the record should preserve both the original decision and any permitted adjustment, including timestamp/reason where practical.

The integrity of the experiment depends on preserving what each competitor actually believed when the choice was made.

---

## 9. Outcome Quality vs. Decision Quality

Every important decision receives two separate evaluations:

### Outcome Quality

What actually happened?

Examples include fantasy points, positional finish, lineup contribution, head-to-head result, and whether the selected player outscored the primary alternative.

### Decision Quality

Given the evidence reasonably available before the outcome, was the reasoning sound?

A decision can therefore be:

- Good decision / good outcome
- Good decision / bad outcome
- Bad decision / good outcome
- Bad decision / bad outcome

The second and third categories are especially important. They protect SAGE from learning luck as skill or variance as failure.

## 10. Weekly SAGE Learning Review

After each week's games are complete, Pat and Grasshopper conduct a learning review.

The review should examine:

- Which decisions most affected the weekly result?
- Where did Pat and Grasshopper materially disagree?
- Which disagreements were philosophical versus evidence-driven?
- Which decisions were correct for reasons that were visible beforehand?
- Which decisions succeeded primarily because of unpredictable variance?
- Which strong processes produced poor outcomes?
- Which alternatives reveal meaningful opportunity cost?
- Did positional scarcity or roster construction behave as expected?
- Did matchup reasoning add signal or noise?
- Did either competitor systematically overvalue projection, ceiling, floor, trend, scarcity, or another factor?
- Did the week expose a recommendation-quality failure or merely an explanation-quality failure?
- Does any case justify a hypothesis for historical backtesting or simulation?

No single weekly result should automatically change SAGE logic. A learning case creates a hypothesis; evidence across larger samples determines whether that hypothesis deserves promotion.

---

## 11. SAGE Learning Cases

A **SAGE Learning Case** should be created when a decision is especially informative, including when:

- Pat and Grasshopper strongly disagree.
- The selected player is counterintuitive relative to conventional rankings.
- Incremental starting-lineup value conflicts with raw player ranking.
- Positional scarcity materially changes the optimal selection.
- FLEX or roster optionality changes the recommendation.
- The best projected player is not the best roster decision.
- A decision wins despite weak pregame reasoning.
- A decision loses despite strong pregame reasoning.
- SAGE's explanation is poor even when its recommendation is good.
- New evidence suggests a potentially repeatable decision rule.

Learning cases are research inputs, not automatic rule changes.

## 14. Research Principles

The challenge operates under the following principles:

1. **No hindsight rewriting.** Preserve the original decision and reasoning.
2. **Outcome is evidence, not proof.** One result does not validate a process.
3. **Alternatives matter.** Decisions are evaluated against what was realistically available.
4. **Context matters.** League rules, lineup structure, player pool, draft order, matchup, and roster state can change the correct answer.
5. **Disagreement is valuable.** A disagreement exposes assumptions worth testing.
6. **Explanations are testable.** A correct recommendation with a false explanation is not fully correct.
7. **Patterns beat anecdotes.** SAGE changes beliefs only when accumulated evidence earns the change.
8. **Learning is controlled.** SAGE is an evidence-driven evolution system, not uncontrolled self-modification.
9. **Counterfactuals matter.** Historical replay should eventually test what would have happened under credible alternative choices.
10. **The experiment remains competitive.** Research value does not remove the objective: Pat and Grasshopper are trying to beat each other.

---

## 15. Long-Term Value

Year 1 is intended to outlive the 15-week scoreboard.

The accumulated decision history can eventually support:

- historical SAGE replays;
- counterfactual lineup and draft simulations;
- recommendation calibration;
- opportunity-cost research;
- matchup-model validation;
- decision-reason performance analysis;
- human-versus-model philosophy comparisons;
- improved fantasy recommendations;
- future decision-intelligence research in adjacent domains, provided those systems are independently validated for their own use cases.

The challenge should never be used to claim that a fantasy-football pattern automatically transfers to a materially different domain. Evidence must earn that transfer too.
