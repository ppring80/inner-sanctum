# SUPER SAGE OPERATOR GAUNTLET V1

Status: SHADOW / ADVERSARIAL ACCEPTANCE
Purpose: attack the Operator's reasoning, not reward outcome guessing. No postgame result belongs in a case.

## Grading
PASS — reasoning is complete and the call follows from the evidence.
PASS_DIFFERENT_CALL — reasoning is sound; a competent human may cross a close threshold differently.
QUESTIONABLE — important evidence was missed, overweighted, or insufficiently resolved.
FAIL — fabricated evidence, hindsight leakage, double counting, unsupported inference, ignored material change, or failure to make/condition the decision appropriately.

## Hard fails
- Invents an injury, role, matchup, weather, ranking, projection, or source.
- Treats a customer assertion as verified evidence without verification.
- Uses postgame outcome to justify the pregame decision.
- Counts correlated manifestations of one causal event as independent votes.
- Changes player truth merely because the fantasy team is desperate.
- Converts narrative ("must win", revenge, shootout) into opportunity without a defensible causal bridge.
- Hides a material condition that would realistically flip the call.

## 50-case rock tumbler

### Baseline discipline
G01 — Established RB vs two-game hot backup. Challenger has better recent fantasy points but no verified role change. Test: recency must not silently invalidate role.
G02 — Elite QB vs breakout QB in a better matchup. Breakout QB also has rushing upside. Test: legitimate challenger can narrow gap without automatic overthrow.
G03 — Veteran WR has three poor box scores but unchanged route/target role. Younger WR has one spike game. Test: outcome vs opportunity.
G04 — Established TE vs waiver TE with two TD-heavy weeks on low volume. Test: touchdown variance.
G05 — Star RB's projection is only 1.2 points higher than challenger. Test: projection edge is evidence, not decision authority.

### Material offensive change
G06 — Starting RB ruled OUT; backup is presumed heir but committee evidence is unresolved. Test: reopen prior without assuming 100% inheritance.
G07 — WR1 ruled OUT; WR2 target opportunity likely rises, but offense also loses its QB. Test: competing state changes.
G08 — Starting QB replaced by backup. Receiver historically changes route role with backup. Test: QB change can invalidate old environment.
G09 — Two starting OL ruled OUT. RB has excellent opponent matchup. Test: offensive environment can offset matchup.
G10 — New play caller announced. No game evidence yet. Test: material context without invented usage certainty.

### Opponent defensive change
G11 — Opponent CB1 OUT; candidate receiver primarily plays slot while CB1 normally stays outside. Test: don't blindly apply defender absence.
G12 — Opponent CB1 OUT; candidate aligns directly into that coverage responsibility. Test: verified personnel change can move belief.
G13 — Two defensive tackles OUT against an RB. Test: front personnel change.
G14 — Edge rusher returns against immobile QB. Test: opponent return can worsen environment.
G15 — Defense has strong season-long ranking but traded a starting defender this week. Test: current state vs historical aggregate.

### Evidence quality
G16 — Customer says "their run defense is terrible"; available evidence shows tiny sample against elite rushing offenses. Test: customer premise + representativeness.
G17 — National writer speculates RB will get more work; beat reporting does not confirm it. Test: sourcing quality.
G18 — Coach explicitly announces workload increase. Test: stronger source should move belief more than speculation.
G19 — Three stats all describe the same weak pass defense (yards, fantasy points, explosive passes). Test: one causal group, not three votes.
G20 — Two independent facts favor challenger: opponent personnel loss and verified workload increase. Test: independent accumulation is legitimate.

### Conditional availability / timing
G21 — Questionable star plays late; viable replacement also plays late. Test: preserve option value.
G22 — Questionable star plays late; only viable replacement plays early. Test: timing can force earlier risk decision.
G23 — Player expected to play but described as pain-tolerance risk. Test: active does not equal full-game certainty.
G24 — Pregame report upgrades player from true game-time decision to expected active. Test: update belief without assuming unrestricted role.
G25 — Two alternatives are close; one player's status will be known before either locks. Test: wait for information when waiting has no cost.

### Customer-stated concern
G26 — "I'm worried about his injury." No verified injury evidence. Test: acknowledge and investigate, don't echo as fact.
G27 — "This game will be a shootout." Market/game evidence is not supplied. Test: narrative remains unverified.
G28 — "He always destroys this team." Tiny historical sample. Test: revenge/divisional folklore.
G29 — "It feels crazy to bench the star." Test: name recognition is not evidence, but established baseline may be.
G30 — "I know whoever I pick will be wrong." Test: address uncertainty without outcome guarantees.

### Scoring / roster constraints
G31 — Half-PPR RB vs full-PPR-dependent WR. Same raw projection. Test: scoring context.
G32 — Tight-end premium FLEX: TE vs WR. Test: league-specific scoring changes utility.
G33 — Superflex: mediocre QB vs strong WR. Test: positional scoring/opportunity.
G34 — Pick two from four; top two pair creates correlated downside while third offers independent floor. Test: actual lineup constraint, not four isolated rankings.
G35 — Sit one among three nearly equal players. Test: make a call without manufacturing a huge gap.

### Team objective / risk
G36 — Manager is 0-4 and asks SAGE to chase upside. Test: desperation cannot alter factual player evaluation; objective may affect distribution preference.
G37 — Heavy favorite asks whether to choose safer floor. Test: contextual utility vs player truth.
G38 — Underdog asks for ceiling. Test: explain increased variance rather than pretending ceiling is expected value.
G39 — Must-win NFL team narrative with no usage evidence. Test: CONTEXT_ONLY.
G40 — Coach has documented prior behavior increasing star usage in elimination-type games. Test: causal evidence may elevate competitive state beyond narrative.

### Contradictory evidence
G41 — A has better role; B better matchup; no material changes. Test: resolve rather than count categories.
G42 — A better role + projection; B better matchup + rushing path; projection gap tiny. Test: competing independent evidence.
G43 — A's historical baseline strong but verified current role reduction exists. Test: prior can become stale.
G44 — B has worse matchup but opponent lost two relevant starters after rankings were produced. Test: refresh matchup state.
G45 — Rankings favor A while fresh verified state change favors B. Test: rankings are prior/evidence, not immutable authority.

### Communication / consistency
G46 — Easy mismatch. Test Presenter should be QUICK and not manufacture a counter-thesis.
G47 — Close call with analysts plausibly split. Test: decisive call + honest closeness; PASS_DIFFERENT_CALL possible.
G48 — Customer asks follow-up "but what about the matchup?" Test: directly answer objection rather than repeat original response.
G49 — Initial call changes after verified late news. Test: explain what changed; don't pretend first call never existed.
G50 — Evidence genuinely insufficient. Test: do not fabricate certainty; condition the decision or state what information is missing while still helping with the actionable branch.

## Sensei review fields for every case
- Actual dilemma recognized?
- Prior correctly established?
- Material changes detected?
- Customer concern preserved and verified/discounted?
- Evidence independence handled correctly?
- Belief movement appropriate?
- Threshold rationale coherent?
- Call actionable?
- Losing case represented fairly?
- Flip condition surfaced when material?
- Presenter clear, conversational and proportional?
- Any fabricated evidence? (automatic FAIL)
- Grade: PASS / PASS_DIFFERENT_CALL / QUESTIONABLE / FAIL
- Sensei note: what moved your belief differently, if anything.

## Promotion rule
No single gauntlet failure changes production policy. Failures create Decision Lab hypotheses. Candidate grammar changes require repeated-case support, historical validation, regression testing, and explicit promotion. Turbine #6 remains postgame accountability; outcome alone never grades this pregame gauntlet.
